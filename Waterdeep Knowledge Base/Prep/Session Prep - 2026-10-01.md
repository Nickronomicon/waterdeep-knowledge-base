# Session Prep - 2026-10-01

Session 17 is a shortened 3.5-hour session starting late. The goal is not a major fight. The goal is to cleanly exit the [[Sessions/Session 16 - 2026-09-24|Session 16]] wagon-extraction aftermath, explain the campaign's heist procedure to the players, and let the party gather information toward a likely heist.

## Session Shape

| Time | Segment | Goal |
|---:|---|---|
| 0:00-0:10 | Player-facing heist structure briefing | Explain the procedure before it becomes urgent. |
| 0:10-0:45 | Watch wagon aftermath | Bodies, statements, Watch heat, no new combat unless players force it. |
| 0:45-1:10 | Return toward Trollskull / messages | Remallia's raven arrives; choose next lead. |
| 1:10-2:20 | Information gathering | Zord/Sea Maidens lead, House of Inspired Hands fallback, or Cassalanter summons. |
| 2:20-3:10 | Main social scene | Zord meeting or Ammalia Cassalanter pitch. |
| 3:10-3:30 | Lock the next operation | Identify score, known facts, needed floorplan/surveillance, player prep declarations if ready. |

Keep the table moving. If a scene is mostly procedural, give one meaningful choice, one clue, and move on.

## Start Here

Opening image: the Watch wagon is gone. Its tracks and panic cut across the street. Two Watch guards are dead, the Zhent spellcaster is dead, at least one thug is dead, and the city is beginning to notice that a prison break just happened in daylight.

Default cleanup choices:

- No fresh combat at the top unless the party chases immediately.
- If a living attacker is useful, leave one badly wounded thug unconscious and stable enough to answer one or two questions before Watch takes him.
- Watch reinforcements arrive fast enough to stop a chase from being clean, but not fast enough to erase the party's agency.
- The party's best legal position is true and simple: they saw the ambush, attacked the ambushers, killed some of them, and failed to stop the wagon.

## Player-Facing Heist Structure Briefing

Read or paraphrase before resuming play:

> We are getting close to parts of the campaign that may work like heists. Not every lead is a heist, and you may not always know you are in one yet, but when you decide there is a specific thing you want from a specific place or person, we will use a loose structure.
>
> First, you identify the score: what you want and where you think it is. That might be an object, a record, a person, a map, or proof.
>
> Second, you gather information. This is the research and legwork phase: asking people questions, calling in faction favors, checking archives, learning schedules, finding entrances, and figuring out who owns what. This can draw attention.
>
> Third, you can do onsite surveillance. That means watching the place physically or magically, casing entrances, tracking guards, looking for routines, and refining what you know. This is usually louder and riskier than research.
>
> Fourth, before the operation, each character can name one skill or ability they are using to prepare. You do not have to describe the full prep right away. Just tell me the kind of preparation your character is leaning into: Investigation, Deception, Athletics, Arcana, Persuasion, thieves' tools, and so on.
>
> Fifth, during the operation, when an obstacle appears, you can reveal what that preparation actually was. Maybe you bribed a dockhand, practiced a climb, forged a delivery order, studied a spell ward, bought a costume, or mapped a patrol route. Then we roll the skill you declared to see how well that prep helps.
>
> Failed rolls should usually create complications, heat, costs, or hard choices, not simply make the whole plan evaporate. The point is to let you make a plan, adapt under pressure, and feel like clever criminals or investigators instead of guessing the one correct door.

DM-only handling:

- Ask for one declared prep skill or ability per PC once the table has a score and basic target.
- During the operation, invite a flashback-style explanation when that prep becomes relevant.
- Roll the declared prep check with advantage as a reward for good description, but do not advertise that ahead of time.
- Keep boundaries: prep can solve plausible obstacles, create contacts, establish tools, or soften consequences. It cannot retcon impossible access, erase known major security, or create expensive/special magic without cost.
- If the target changes, let players revise declarations before the operation begins.

## Scene 1: Watch Aftermath

Purpose: prevent the wagon escape from feeling consequence-free without trapping the whole session in legal procedure.

Watch arrival options:

| Watch Face | Use If | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| [[People/NPCs/Saeth Cromley]] | You want continuity from the Fireball scene. | Suspicious, tired, but sees the party fought the ambushers. |
| [[People/NPCs/Barnibus Blastwind]] | You want magic forensics and dry pressure. | Quietly notes spells, bodies, and Aladeen's prior Frewn rumor. |
| [[People/NPCs/Hustus Staget]] | You want a sterner official warning. | This is no longer tavern trouble; dead Watch changes the temperature. |

Useful Watch questions:

- Who started the fight?
- Who was driving the wagon when it fled?
- Did anyone hear a name?
- Did the escaped prisoner say anything?
- Why were the Skullduggers in the middle of this again?
- Did anyone search the dead Zhents or move evidence?

Clues available from the scene:

- The spellcaster has a flying-snake tattoo and corpse-like pallor; she was not a random street tough.
- The name "Agorn" is now table-revealed as the driver/leader.
- The attackers wanted Urstul alive and mobile, not dead.
- If searched before Watch locks down the scene, the spellcaster has components and a small amount of coin, but no full lair map.
- If a wounded thug is questioned: "Agorn said Floxin could not reach the tower." He does not know the Stone, but he knows the order came through the same network as Yellowspire.

Exit ramp:

- Watch takes over the bodies and tells the party to stay reachable at Trollskull.
- If the party is cooperative, Watch lets them go after statements because their testimony and the dead Zhents make the basic story plausible.
- If they hide evidence or get belligerent, Staget or Cromley warns that Trollskull is becoming a pattern.

## Scene 2: Remallia's Silver Raven

Deliver once the party is out of immediate Watch procedure or when they return toward Trollskull.

Message content:

> Of the nimblewrights in the city, only eight known examples match Luskan construction. All eight were sold through Zardoz Zord. I do not yet have the buyer names. Be careful: asking after them openly may tell the seller exactly what you know.

Use this to push the party toward [[People/NPCs/Zardoz Zord]] without forcing the route.

If they ask Remallia for more:

- She can confirm Zord is socially slippery and useful, not a normal shopkeeper.
- She does not have a safe buyer list.
- She recommends either getting Zord to volunteer the list or learning where he keeps sales records.
- She will not send Harper agents into the Sea Maidens Fair blind on the party's behalf, but she can quietly receive anything they learn.

## Main Lead A: Zardoz And The Customer List

Use if the party follows the raven clue, the Luskan nimblewright lead, or the Sea Maidens Fair.

### Approach Choices

| Approach | What Happens |
|---|---|
| Request a meeting | Zord meets them in the cabin of one of the moored ships, not the Eyecatcher. He is amused, theatrical, and deeply interested in what they know. |
| Ask dock workers first | They can learn Zord controls the fair's operation, the Eyecatcher stays offshore, and ship access is socially managed. |
| Try to sneak immediately | Treat this as premature onsite surveillance. They can learn movement patterns and ship access, but the target is not mapped yet. |
| Go through Captain Fergus | Reuse the retconned Session 15 branch as fresh table truth: Fergus is drunk, performative, and can get a message to Zord. |

### Zord's Social Position

Zord will identify the Gralhunds as the relevant owners only if the party tells him enough to make the trade worthwhile: Fireball, red-caped nimblewright, Dalakhar, Neverember angle, and why the buyer list matters.

If the party withholds too much:

- Zord confirms he sold Luskan-style nimblewrights in Waterdeep.
- He does not hand over buyer names for free.
- He lets slip, or allows them to infer, that records exist and are not kept casually in a public stall.
- End with the clear possible score: the nimblewright customer list.

### Heist Framing For Zord Route

| Phase | Current State |
|---|---|
| Identify the score | Nimblewright buyer/customer list, especially the owner of the red-cloaked bright-hatted construct. |
| Gather information | Who keeps the records? Which ship? Who has keys? Does Zord travel between ships by boat, magic, or schedule? |
| Onsite surveillance | Watch docked ships, crew rotations, carnival traffic, access to cabins, messenger traffic to the Eyecatcher. |
| Prep work | Disguises, forged delivery orders, climbing/swimming route, social invitation, distraction, magical detection, escape route. |
| Operation | Probably next session unless the table races straight into it. |

Good session-ending beat: the party has the score, a partial floorplan or ship target, and each player has named a prep skill.

## Main Lead B: A Request From Victoro Cassalanter

Use if the party does not pursue Zord promptly, if you need a strong social scene, or if the session needs a non-heist revelation lane.

Hook:

- A Cassalanter messenger reaches Trollskull or intercepts the party after the Watch aftermath.
- Victoro requests that the Skullduggers visit [[Places/Cassalanter Villa]] as soon as possible. Public reason: he has heard about the Fireball and wants reassurance that his investment remains sound.
- At the villa, [[People/NPCs/Ammalia Cassalanter]] receives them instead.

Ammalia's reveal list:

- The Cassalanter twins, Terenzio and Elzerina, are doomed on their ninth birthday.
- Their birthday is ten days after Founders' Day, Tarsahk 11.
- Enough money can save them, but the family needs it by Leiruin Feast, Tarsahk 9.
- Dagult Neverember hid 500,000 embezzled gold dragons somewhere in Waterdeep.
- The [[Objects/Stone of Golorr]] is rumored to be the key to that treasure.
- The Cassalanters were following a gnome named Dalakhar because they suspected he carried the Stone.

Do not reveal yet:

- The Cassalanters' Eye.
- The full infernal bargain structure beyond the sympathetic rescue frame.
- Any direct confirmation of cult operations.

Elior's scar:

- Low baseline heat throughout Cassalanter Villa.
- No flare when Ammalia speaks about protecting her children; that motive is not, by itself, against the natural order.
- If infernal architecture, ritual space, or contract language comes close, increase heat slightly but keep it ambiguous unless they push.

Ammalia's ask:

- Find the Stone before the factions killing in the streets do.
- Share what they know about Dalakhar, the nimblewright, and Neverember.
- If they recover the Stone, bring it to people with the resources to turn it into rescue, not gang profit.

## Secrets And Clues To Reveal

Prioritize these tonight:

- Only eight known Luskan-design nimblewrights are in Waterdeep, and all were sold by Zardoz Zord.
- Zord's buyer records are enough to identify the Gralhunds if the party reaches them socially or by heist.
- The Stone of Golorr is now ready to become player-facing if the Cassalanter scene occurs.
- The Fireball was not random gang violence; factions are fighting over a specific missing object and records around it.
- Asking questions is now noisy. Each information-gathering step can draw faction attention.
- Heists are not punishments for choosing stealth; they are a supported play structure.

## Foundry / Table Assets

Prepare only what tonight likely needs:

- A blank heist-planning scene the players can mark up once a target is chosen.
- A simple Dock Ward / Sea Maidens Fair splash or pier map if they go to Zord.
- A moored ship cabin scene for a Zord meeting.
- Cassalanter Villa social scene image/map if they take the request.
- Handout or pinned note summarizing the five heist phases.
- Optional customer-list placeholder handout; do not reveal contents until earned.

## End States To Aim For

Any one of these is a successful shortened session:

- The party chooses Zord as the next target and identifies the customer list as the score.
- The party meets Zord, earns or fails to earn the Gralhund clue, and considers heisting the records.
- The party hears Ammalia's Cassalanter pitch and finally gets the Stone/vault stakes in plain language.
- The party names a heist target, has a rough information plan, and each player declares a prep skill for the future operation.
