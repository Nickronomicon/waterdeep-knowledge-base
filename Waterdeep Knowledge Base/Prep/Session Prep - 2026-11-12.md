# Session Prep - 2026-11-12

**Three hours; voice and Discord; five level-3 PCs.** Tonight's score is identifying the buyer of the Fireball nimblewright through Zord's ledger. Prepare the ship's people and obstacles; let the players choose the approach. A satisfying endpoint is the **Gralhund name, a consequential escape, and a next destination**. Getting the information through a bargain also counts.

**All new schedules, security placement, dialogue, DCs, and handout wording below are session proposals, not played facts.** The ledger/Gralhund answer and Zelifarn's stolen crystal-ball history come from the Remix. Keep discoveries out of current-state/entity pages until played.

For the minimum reading, use [[Prep/Session 19 - Quick Run Sheet]]. Handout release instructions are below; each player file is plain Markdown, individually under 2,000 characters. Copy only the selected file's body into Discord. No VTT, map image, or bot is required.

Choose one useful scene rather than requiring every row. Obvious observations after time spent watching need no roll. Roll only for risky or unusually detailed access; failure adds an exposed face, a favor, or lost time, not false essential information.

## Zelifarn — A Useful Ally With His Own Score

Use [[People/NPCs/Zelifarn]]: curious, likes stories and treasures, friendly to the party, recognizes Broethuul's wrong-water scent. He remembers their visit; no new trust test just to talk. Let him surface by their boat after they call/seek him near his known wreck. **This encounter is a proposed convenience, not evidence he was waiting there last session.**

Original opening: “You have that look again. The look people have before something falls off a boat.”

**Remix motive:** Jarlaxle and his people killed Zelifarn's mother, **Asphosis**, and stole her crystal ball; it is kept in the **Scarlet Marpenoth**, a concealed vessel under Eyecatcher. The dragon wants it recovered. These are his account and a possible **later score**, not another task required before tonight's ledger. He does not know the ball's full surveillance function. A bronze dragon's curiosity can turn serious when his mother comes up.

Start with what they request. If asked why he watches the ship or what he wants, give the motive honestly. If asked who stole it, name Jarlaxle; the recommended source-faithful version lets him identify Zord as that person. This may reveal a major identity tonight; record it if it happens. Do not assume this knowledge in the initial Discord recap. Do not force the crystal-ball heist into the remaining two hours.

| Requested Help | Proposed Offer | Limit / Price |
|---|---|---|
| Diversion | A conspicuous surfacing or circling wake draws bow watcher and mate forward for **three exploration beats** once signaled. Stern sailor stays at post. | Promise a serious conversation about recovering his mother's crystal, not immediate recovery as a condition. He will not sink innocent ships or fire lightning through crew. |
| Approach / pickup | Tow their small boat near the stern or meet a swimmer at an agreed offshore point. | Name signal, pickup point, waiting time. Breathing is not supplied by riding him. He cannot be everywhere at once. |
| Underwater reconnaissance | Describes a metal craft attached beneath the hull and an access tube. | This reveals a tempting new route and his grievance, not a free entrance into the office or a complete submarine plan. |
| Full battle | He declines to wage their fight or expose the harbor to destruction. | Friendly refusal; mundane approaches still work. Persuasion can improve a bounded favor, not enslave him. |

A fair, thoughtful request and honest commitment can secure one favor without a roll. If they want both transport and diversion, propose Persuasion DC 14 and a specific return favor; on failure he offers one, not neither. This DC and timing are prep rulings. A “whale diversion” means rolling/wakes or a disguise provided by PCs; **do not give a young bronze dragon an unestablished shapechanging ability**. With both duties, he diverts first, then needs one beat to reach pickup.

## The Ship In Six Places

**Campaign adaptation:** original Chapter 7 puts the ordinary captain's cabin at J10 and Jarlaxle's ledger office at J30 elsewhere. Session 18 physically established the office as an aftcastle cabin reached across the main deck. For tonight, put the **ledger and relevant J30 features in that known cabin footprint**. Do not require players to find a different office. Other interior connections below are new proposed staging. Published keys are DM cross-references, not clues the players possess.

```text
WATER / SMALL BOAT — MAIN DECK — STERN LANDING — OFFICE / LEDGER
                        |             |              |
                  BOW / RIGGING   SERVICE PASSAGE — WARDROBE
                                                      |
                                       office rear window → WATER
```

These are connections, not a scale map. Bow and stern are 60 feet apart along the exposed deck; landing and office threshold are within 15 feet. Office is about 20 feet across. Set the rear window 10 feet above the water, broad enough for a person, with a narrow ledge; these dimensions are session staging, not measurements from the book.

| Place | Describe / Use | Initial Opposition |
|---|---|---|
| Water / small boat | Slap of water under black hull; rope ladder amidships, narrow stern ledge beneath window. | Bow watcher can see the ordinary approach; stern sailor can check boats at the rear. |
| Main deck / bow | Coiled lines, stacked boats, cargo hatch, masts; crates give partial visual cover. | Bow watcher + amidships sailor. Rigging itself is climbable; exposed movement is the risk. |
| Stern landing | Short stair, covered office entrance, bell-rope running toward watch station. | Stern sailor; mate passes every third exploration beat. |
| Service passage | Pantry/laundry activity and doors used by crew. | Two off-watch crew inside, initially busy; unfamiliar faces need a reason. No automatic identity check from across a closed door. |
| Wardrobe | Clothes, towels, a seam in the wall to the office. Access from service passage. | Outer door locked DC 15; connecting panel is unwarded. A careful search finds it; DC 13 finds it before someone comes past. |
| Office | Desk and genuine ledger; purple drapes, chess pieces, silent clockwork attendant. Rear window overlooks water. | Exterior door locked DC 15 and magically announced; attendant guards private floor hatch. |

Ask where everyone is **before** a check. Each player can be “in the boat,” “on the deck,” “at the office,” etc. At every cutaway repeat position, immediate threat, and exit. Alternate infiltration and support groups every 5–10 real minutes; one risky task per spotlight before switching.

## Fixed Security And Alert

**Watch roster:** W1 bow; W2 amidships; W3 stern; M1 mate patrol. Mark these four on paper. Most crew remain below, explaining the earlier sighting of 16–20 people and estimated larger complement; do not replace them with four total sailors. Two additional responders emerge **two beats after full alarm**, announced by boots on stairs. One beat is one significant exploration task, roughly a minute; in combat use rounds and say when conversion happens.

**Office door ward:** keep the original magic-mouth idea. Turning either **exterior office door handle** without Zord triggers a loud spoken demand. A copied key opens the lock but does not defeat the ward. It is a local alarm heard by nearby watch, not telepathic tracking. Handles have a small sculpted face and hairline magical script; anyone carefully examining them sees this. Detect Magic/Arcana DC 13 identifies the trigger before touching. Dispel Magic, bypassing the handle, wardrobe panel, window, or an applicable declared prep can solve it. A ward sounding raises Alert to **at least 2**, not instant capture. Last visit was authorized with Zord inside; do not infer the PCs have already heard/disabled it.

**Window:** latched from inside. A close look notices it; thieves' tools DC 13 through the gap opens it. Breaking glass works with noise; no second lock. The stern watcher must be distracted, evaded, or dealt with first.

**Nimblewright:** the attendant notices obvious intruders but its standing order is to prevent access to the private floor hatch, **not to attack everyone who reads paperwork** (original J30 behavior). It follows a hatch-searcher and interposes; on persistent access/attack it fights and alerts nearby crew. Its face/attire differs from the bearded, hat-wearing Fireball culprit. It has no spoken passwords, can be deceived by a plausible authorized order at DC 15, and cannot see the whole ship. Do not make it omniscient through the separate crystal ball.

| Alert | Visible Change | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Routine | Ordinary calls and work. | Listed watch positions. |
| 1 — Uneasy | A lantern turns; a sailor asks a specific question. | Investigate the actual sound/person. A plausible explanation or hiding can end the immediate pursuit. |
| 2 — Searching | Mate summoned; stern access challenged. | Mate heads to last known location; watches narrow their attention. Ward alone can produce this stage. |
| 3 — Alarm | Bell, shouted positions, boats being readied. | Guards block known exits, pursue known intruders, offer surrender. Two responders in two beats; shore launch/authority help takes time. |

Raise by one for a witnessed intrusion, an unaddressed suspicious failure, or obvious assault; a loud ongoing fight reaches 3. Don't advance for harmless bad rolls, ordinary planning, or every minute. Explain what exposed them. A successful distraction moves guards to its source; it does not erase someone they already recognized. **No secretly prearranged “Jarlaxle allowed it” ending.** On a clean success they genuinely beat security.

## Preparation And Rules Without Foundry

Post Handout 03. Preserve [[Reference/Remix Eye Heists|the announced campaign procedure]]: each PC declares **one skill, tool, ability, or spell** before the operation. When it matters, they describe the earlier work and roll then. This does not grant a new spell, free costly item, or changed resource total. Let support PCs use preparations from shore. Particularly strong, specific descriptions can earn advantage under the established DM approach.

Examples: forge a supply order; practice the stern climb; arrange a rower; learn a watch call; have a costume ready. Decide any ordinary cost before the flashback takes effect. Simple declared preparations can work without checks if there is no uncertain obstacle. A player who legitimately bought a tool does not roll to discover whether they brought it.

For spells, read their sheet wording aloud; retain range, duration, components, concentration, and target limits. Invisibility is not silence or automatic concealment. A noisy spell is noisy; do not erase guards' chance to respond. Darkness/illusions change what guards perceive without providing universal invisibility. No mandatory Arcana roll merely to use a spell normally.

**Theater-of-the-mind movement:** exploration uses connected places. In initiative return to actual movement/ranges: adjacent landing/doorway within one move, bow-to-stern 60 feet may take Dash, office 20 feet across. State who is engaged, distances to exits, and which enemies an area spell can catch **before** the player commits. Do not charge an action solely to change a zone if remaining movement already covers the distance. A normal jump overboard into reachable water needs no check; darkness, wounds, current, or pursuit can make swimming/boarding uncertain. Announce that risk first.

## Ledger: Pay Off Reaching The Desk

**It is the real book, written in readable Common, with an identifying illustration/description and delivery notes.** Once they can examine it, they find the matching entry without a pass/fail Investigation gate. The core reward is not hidden behind one last roll.

Proposed timing: identify and jot the key buyer/address in one exploration beat; copy that whole entry in two; copy all sales records in ten; carry the whole ledger as an object interaction and leave a conspicuous gap. In combat a hurried reader uses one action to find the prominent illustrated entry and a second to transcribe the name/address; take the book if time is tighter. Explain the choice and approaching footsteps before deciding. A relevant literacy/language ability can alter this; do not add cipher work unexpectedly.

Release **Handout 05 only after they read the entry**, whichever route got them there. They learn **House Gralhund, Saerdoun Street, North Ward**, matching the red cape/hat/carved beard. This proves a purchase/delivery connection, not an order to commit murder or the Stone's current location. They recognize the Gralhund name from the opera if reminded; no memory roll needed for that recap.

Handout is an excerpt, not the entire buyer roster. It makes no eight-versus-nine claim and does not retroactively declare every campaign owner a Zord customer. If they take/copy everything, they possess the rest of the ledger for later scrutiny; summarize routine records now and preserve it as inventory. Any new non-core buyer entries should be reconciled with Davin Crommor, Bojentra, and the campaign's other leads before a full-list handout later.

Optional extra for a careful search: diagrams of a metal access tube or an old scale caught by the hatch, pointing toward Zelifarn's second score. No automatic Cult of Asmodeus/Grand Game report: Jarlaxle's actual knowledge remains dependent on what he learned. Do not dump missing Eyes or the Cassalanter truth into tonight's ledger.

## Escape, Exposure, And Zord's Bargain

A clear escape with no pursuit needs no compulsory chase. If they have a pickup, use it; if the dragon moved to distract, respect his transit beat. The tap stones work only within **60 feet**, not across the mile to shore.

If pursued, ask for an escape plan and run **two concrete obstacles**: get off the ship, then break pursuit/reach pickup. Each PC gets one contribution, with rolls only where uncertain. A failure costs lost gear chosen by the player, separation, injury with a stated damage/risk, a witness, or a stopped route; it does not automatically put everyone in chains. At full alarm, the known ordinary boat access can be guarded while an unobserved stern window still offers a chance. Do not give the ship instant naval blockade or police teleportation.

If intercepted, Zord learns only what his observers actually saw or reported. He remains ashore until an actual return trip or a separately established ability brings him back. Crew can hold/parley by his standing orders; he is not suddenly in the only unguarded exit.

**Zord wants:** keep identities/underwater assets private; learn why they are chasing this sale; retain commercial and political leverage. His threats are credible, not an obligation to execute thieves. Original dialogue: “You went to considerable trouble for a customer reference. Tell me what makes this buyer worth the swim.”

A concrete proposed bargain: **a truthful account of Dalakhar, the Stone lead, and the family's interest in exchange for the relevant buyer entry and safe departure**. They can refuse, counteroffer a later report/favor, attempt escape, or use leverage. No compulsory oath or geas. He won't volunteer his true identity merely to gloat. If they already read/copied the entry and can escape, don't force a bargain anyway.

Capture is a possible consequence **only if play produces it**. Crew prefer prisoners; melee knockouts are possible, ranged attacks are not automatically nonlethal. If actually captured, negotiate release/information in this session rather than automatically stripping every item and starting a month-long prison arc. Their patrons create bargaining value, not automatic immunity.

Start with **two crew** in the actual location; use the mate if already present. Others take the announced travel time. Their goal is to stop intruders, ring the bell, or hold a doorway. PCs can spend turns reading, opening an exit, negotiating, or escaping; killing all opposition isn't the victory condition.

Resists nonmagical weapon bludgeoning/piercing/slashing; immune to exhaustion, frightened, petrified, poisoned. Advantage on saves vs magic and against being knocked prone.
